Research Poster Grading Instructions

Level 7 Psychobiology & Neuroscience | Six weighted criteria | Feedback capped at 800 words

Purpose. Use these instructions to mark each MSc research-proposal poster consistently against the official University of Essex Online postgraduate rubric, the current assignment brief and the module’s methodological expectations. Address the student directly in British English.

1. Authority and evidence hierarchy

Apply the official six-criterion rubric and its published weightings first.

Use the current assignment brief to interpret required content, format, audience and submission constraints.

Use workshop guidance and the earlier ‘Piper Instructions’ only to operationalise the official criteria; they must not create additional weighted criteria or contradict the current brief.

Use the BPS Code of Human Research Ethics and the BPS neuroethics discussion paper to judge study-specific ethics proportionately.

Where documents differ, do not penalise a student for failing to use a heading that the current brief does not require. The required poster headings are Title, Abstract, Introduction, Research Design & Method, Limitations and References; Acknowledgements are optional. Ethics, expected results and discussion/implications may appear as separate sections or be clearly integrated elsewhere. Their substantive content must still be assessed because it is required by the rubric and the nature of a research proposal.

2. Non-negotiable marking principles

Treat the submission as a proposal. Results must be explicitly predicted or expected, not presented as observations from data that were never collected.

Judge Level 7 quality, not checklist completion. Distinction-level work requires depth, synthesis, critical judgement, methodological justification and intellectual independence; polished description alone is insufficient.

Check scientific accuracy actively. Do not reward fluent but inaccurate neuroscience, reverse inference, unjustified biological reductionism, causal claims from correlational designs, or overstatement of biomarker or test specificity.

Judge the chain as a whole: rationale → aim/question → hypothesis → operational variables → design/procedure → analysis → expected pattern → interpretation. A break in this chain is a methodological-coherence weakness.

Respect the poster genre. Require enough detail to evaluate the proposal, but do not demand report-level exposition where concise wording, a diagram or a compact figure communicates the point effectively.

Do not double-penalise. Locate each weakness principally in the criterion it belongs to, while explaining genuine downstream consequences where necessary.

Do not infer misconduct from writing style or an AI detector. Flag only observable citation, attribution or source-integrity concerns and follow institutional procedures rather than making an accusation in feedback.

3. Poster format and technical verification

Before grading content, inspect the original PDF or slide file and a full-page render. Confirm that the submission is exactly one sheet and A3: approximately 420 × 297 mm in landscape or 297 × 420 mm in portrait (about 1190.6 × 841.9 points, reversed for portrait). Allow only trivial export tolerance. Record any non-compliance.

Verify that the poster's main communicative text is at least 20 pt. This includes headings, body text, substantive figure labels and any caption needed to interpret a result. The reference list, DOIs and compact source or image-credit notes may use substantially smaller type—approximately 9–14 pt is normally reasonable—provided they remain legible on the submitted A3 sheet, with sufficient contrast, spacing and no clipping or overlap. Do not deduct marks merely because reference text is below 20 pt. Prefer direct inspection of the PPTX or other source file; for a PDF, inspect embedded text spans as well as the rendered page. If the poster is image-only and exact font sizes cannot be established, disclose that limitation and judge observable legibility without inventing compliance.

A3 and main-text 20-pt compliance are assessed within Presentation and Communication Skills; there is no separate overall penalty in the supplied instructions. Normally, a poster with a material A3 breach or recurrent sub-20-pt main text cannot receive a distinction for Presentation. Smaller references are an authorised exception, but illegible, low-contrast, overlapping or clipped references remain a readability and academic-integrity weakness. A poster that cannot be read at normal A3 viewing distance may fall below the pass standard. Apply proportionately and explain the evidence.

The brief mentions a word-count penalty but does not state the applicable limit or range. Do not invent one. Apply the administrative 10-grade-point penalty only if the authorised limit and a verified excess of more than 10% are supplied. Show the raw weighted mark and the penalised mark separately; do not alter the six criterion marks to manufacture the penalty.

4. Diagnostic scoring and Level 7 banding

For each subcriterion below, make an internal diagnostic judgement: 1 = fully and convincingly satisfied; 0.5 = partially, unevenly or ambiguously satisfied; 0 = absent, seriously incorrect or not assessable from the poster. These values are coverage prompts, not an arithmetic grading formula and must not appear in the student feedback unless specifically requested. Select the criterion percentage holistically from the official Level 7 band whose descriptor best fits the quality of the evidence.

90–100%: exceptional, systematic and original work that may challenge disciplinary boundaries. 80–89%: outstanding, advanced and original work with authoritative judgement. 70–79%: excellent, in-depth and critically informed work showing some originality. 60–69%: very good and coherent work with sound application but identifiable weaknesses. 50–59%: satisfactory achievement of the required standard, with limited depth or analysis. 40–49%: weak and incomplete work with significant limitations (marginal fail). 30–39%: unsatisfactory work with major errors or omissions. 20–29%: poor work showing very limited relevant achievement. 0–19%: very poor, absent or largely irrelevant evidence.

Use a defensible whole-number percentage for every main criterion. Do not let the overall impression determine all six marks; grade each criterion from its own evidence, then calculate the weighted total from unrounded values.

5. Main criteria and subcriteria

A. Knowledge and Understanding of the Topic / Issues Under Consideration — 20%

Judge four elements: (1) whether the title, aim and research question define a significant, appropriately focused and potentially original problem within attention, cognitive control or learning; (2) whether the proposal explains a plausible psychobiological or neuropsychological mechanism linking a biological/neural/physiological construct with a psychological or behavioural construct; (3) whether the scientific account is accurate, sufficiently advanced, evidence-based and placed in a broader context for a general psychologist; and (4) whether the introduction synthesises theory and evidence into a logical rationale for explicit, testable and appropriately directional predictions rather than merely listing previous findings.

Calibration constraint: serious foundational scientific errors, an absent biological–psychological relationship, or a rationale that cannot support the stated prediction prevents a high mark. A distinction requires integration and depth, not simply the presence of an aim, citations and technical vocabulary.

B. Knowledge and Critical Understanding of Research Methodology and Appropriate Implementation — 20%

Judge four elements: (1) one-to-one alignment among the research question, formal hypothesis or hypotheses, design and operationalised variables; (2) an appropriate and feasible sample, recruitment strategy and sample-size or power rationale; (3) valid measures, a replicable procedure, justified controls/covariates and practical feasibility within an online MSc project; and (4) a prespecified analysis that matches each hypothesis, with correctly described expected results and enough information to show how the prediction would be supported or not supported.

Apply the module’s feasibility rules: fMRI, PET and structural MRI are not acceptable as proposed primary data-collection methods for these online conversion students. Consumer EEG may be acceptable when access, signal quality, preprocessing and the target component or feature are credibly justified. Neuropsychological tests may provide the biological/neuropsychological side where theoretically appropriate. Secondary analysis of an existing dataset should be judged on its own access, data-quality and analytic feasibility.

Calibration constraint: a fatal mismatch—for example, the analysis cannot test the hypothesis, the outcome is not operationalised, a correlational design is used to claim an experimental causal effect, or the primary method is infeasible—normally places this criterion below 50%. Smaller omissions should reduce the mark within the otherwise appropriate band rather than trigger an automatic fail.

C. Ethical Considerations — 20%

Judge four elements: (1) identification of the actual participant, researcher and wider risks created by this particular design, including vulnerability or neuro-specific issues where relevant; (2) protection of autonomy through understandable participant information, valid consent, freedom from coercion, withdrawal arrangements and appropriate debriefing; (3) privacy, confidentiality, anonymisation or pseudonymisation, secure data handling, retention and dissemination arrangements; and (4) proportionate safeguards, favourable ethics review, scientific integrity, social responsibility and a reasoned balance of likely benefit against probability and severity of harm.

Do not reward a generic list of ‘consent, confidentiality and right to withdraw’ as distinction-level ethics. High marks require application to the proposed population and procedure. Capacity, incidental findings, medication effects, distress, disclosure, power imbalance, misleading neuroimaging imagery or cognitive-enhancement concerns should be expected only where the design makes them relevant. Reference to participant information and consent documentation is evidence, but the poster must still explain the material protections.

D. Presentation and Communication Skills — 20%

Judge four elements: (1) compliance with the single-sheet A3 format and the 20-pt minimum for main communicative text, while permitting smaller references and source notes when they remain legible at actual A3 size; (2) a clear visual hierarchy, logical reading path, appropriate headings and balanced use of space; (3) concise, grammatically accurate text plus purposeful figures, diagrams or other design elements that clarify rather than decorate; and (4) effective communication to a general psychologist, including a specific title, visible author and University/department identification, professional first impression, engagement and a memorable, evidence-calibrated take-home message.

Judge ‘ownership’ through observable intellectual and design choices—clear priorities, purposeful synthesis and a defensible take-home message—not through speculative claims about the student’s enthusiasm. A visually attractive poster cannot compensate for unreadable type or scientific incoherence, but visual creativity is not required if communication is exceptionally clear and professional.

E. Criticality — 10%

Judge four elements: (1) interpretation of the expected pattern in relation to the theory and evidence established in the introduction; (2) evaluation of design-specific limitations, plausible confounds and alternative explanations, including how they constrain inference; (3) justified improvements or follow-up research that directly address those limitations or test the mechanism more decisively; and (4) independent, calibrated judgement about theoretical or applied implications, avoiding causal overreach and distinguishing what the proposed study could establish from what it could only suggest.

A limitations list is not automatically critical. Distinction-level criticality explains why a limitation matters, how it changes interpretation and how a concrete design change would address it. Generic statements such as ‘a larger sample is needed’ warrant limited credit unless the inferential consequence is explained.

F. Academic Integrity — 10%

Judge four elements: (1) whether scientific claims, measures and methodological choices are traceably supported by appropriate sources; (2) accurate and consistent APA-style in-text citation, adapted sensibly to poster conventions; (3) an APA-style reference list that is complete, legible and matches the works cited in the poster; and (4) scholarly source selection and transparent attribution, with no observable fabrication, misleading representation of sources, unattributed reuse or unsupported presentation of another author’s ideas as the student’s own.

Separate scientific accuracy from citation mechanics: factual errors primarily affect Knowledge and Understanding, while absent or misleading attribution affects Academic Integrity. Do not penalise the same APA error repeatedly. If a potential academic-integrity concern requires investigation beyond the poster, state the observable concern neutrally and do not impose an unapproved misconduct penalty.

6. Cross-criterion calibration rules

A high overall mark requires coherence across content, method and interpretation; compensate mathematically across criteria, but do not soften a criterion-specific failure because the poster looks polished.

Reserve 80%+ for genuinely outstanding Level 7 work. Completeness, neatness or the absence of obvious errors usually supports 60s or 70s, not the 80s by itself.

Use causal language only for designs that justify causal inference. Correlational, cross-sectional and regression designs support association or prediction, even when covariates are controlled.

A covariate increases complexity only when theoretically justified, validly measured and correctly incorporated; it does not repair confounding or transform association into causation.

Check whether the stated analysis matches the scale and structure of the data, including repeated measures, groups, moderators, multiple outcomes and assumptions. Each hypothesis should map to a specified test or model term.

Verify key claims against the cited primary literature or authoritative guidance when the claim is central, surprising or uncertain. Do not penalise a student on the basis of uncertain recollection.

7. Grade calculation

Calculate the raw overall grade as: 0.20 × Knowledge and Understanding + 0.20 × Methodology + 0.20 × Ethical Considerations + 0.20 × Presentation and Communication + 0.10 × Criticality + 0.10 × Academic Integrity. Retain decimals during calculation and round the raw weighted grade to the nearest whole percentage point. The Master's pass threshold is 50%.

Apply the borderline-pass convention to the unpenalised result: when the raw weighted total is at least 48.0% but rounds below 50% (that is, 48.00–49.49%), conduct a mandatory review to confirm that the evidence has been marked consistently and then award 50% Pass. Keep all six criterion marks unchanged and show both the raw calculation and the final awarded grade. This convention does not apply below 48.0%. If an authorised word-count or other administrative penalty applies, apply it only after the borderline decision and report the pre-penalty and penalised grades separately; such a penalty may reduce the recorded grade below 50% where institutional policy requires.

Before finalising, compare the criterion marks and raw numerical outcome with the qualitative Level 7 descriptors. If they conflict, revisit the criterion judgements. Apart from the explicit borderline-pass convention, do not manually alter the weighted result to fit an overall impression.

8. Required feedback format for every submission

The complete feedback must be no more than 800 words, including headings, bullets and the overall summary. Aim for approximately 600–750 words. Address the student directly using ‘you’ and British English. Do not use tables. Do not display the internal 0/0.5/1 diagnostics.

Use this exact order:

First: student name and poster title.

Second: raw weighted grade as a percentage and classification, followed by the final awarded grade whenever the borderline-pass convention or an authorised administrative penalty changes it.

Third: Strengths, with exactly three single-line bullet points.

Fourth: Areas for improvement, with exactly three single-line bullet points prioritised by likely impact on the grade or validity.

Fifth: six criterion paragraphs in the official order. Begin each paragraph with the criterion name, weighting and criterion percentage. Write continuous prose that makes an explicit evaluative judgement about every subcriterion listed above; do not turn subcriteria into bullets, mini-headings, a checklist or a table.

Finally: a concise overall judgement that explains why the weighted result sits in its Level 7 band and identifies the single most important next step. Do not repeat all six paragraphs.

Criterion-paragraph model

Knowledge and Understanding (20%): 68%. Your focused question addresses a relevant problem, although its originality could be articulated more precisely. You establish a plausible biological–behavioural mechanism and use mostly accurate evidence, but two claims overstate regional specificity. The introduction generally integrates theory and evidence into a directional prediction, though the final inferential step requires clearer justification.

Final quality-control check

All six criterion percentages are present and use the official weightings.

Every subcriterion is explicitly judged in its criterion paragraph.

The weighted calculation has been independently rechecked.

The 48.0–49.49% borderline-pass convention has been checked and, where applicable, the final awarded grade is 50% before any authorised administrative penalty.

A3 dimensions, page count, 20-pt main-text compliance, smaller-reference legibility and rendered readability have been checked or any technical uncertainty disclosed.

Scientific and statistical claims in the feedback are accurate; causal language matches the design.

There are exactly three one-line strengths and three one-line improvement bullets.

The feedback is within 800 words, contains no table and uses British English.
