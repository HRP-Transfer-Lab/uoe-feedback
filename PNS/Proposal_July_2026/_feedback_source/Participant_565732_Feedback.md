## Knowledge and Understanding of the Topic / Issues — 68%

You show a sound understanding of sleep continuity, executive control and selective attention, and the choice to measure sleep efficiency objectively makes the proposal clearly psychobiological. The discussion of slow-wave sleep, frontal functioning and cognitive control provides a plausible mechanism, while the review of sex differences gives a reason to consider moderation. The sex-moderation rationale remains relatively broad and the hypothesis does not specify the direction of the interaction because the literature is inconsistent. That is defensible, but you should make the theoretical uncertainty explicit and distinguish confirmatory from exploratory aspects of the moderator test.

## Research Methodology and Appropriate Implementation — 63%

The observational correlational design is appropriate, and the key variables are well operationalised. Seven nights of actigraphy are averaged into sleep efficiency, Flanker interference is calculated from incongruent minus congruent reaction time, and multiple regression with an interaction tests moderation by biological sex. Feasibility also needs attention: providing the same standardised actigraphy device to enough participants for seven consecutive nights can require substantial equipment and scheduling capacity. Explain how many devices are available, how monitoring will be staggered, and how missing nights or non-wear will be handled.

## Ethical Considerations — 61%

You address informed consent, voluntary withdrawal, confidentiality, minimal risk and the important point that research sleep data are not diagnostic. That is appropriate. Actigraphy also creates practical responsibilities around device loss, hygiene and return. These are manageable issues but should be visible in a complete protocol.

## Criticality — 61%

Your limitations are thoughtful. You distinguish association from causation, identify stress, mood, health and physical activity as possible common causes, explain that actigraphy does not provide polysomnographic sleep-stage information, and recognise that a sex difference cannot establish a hormonal mechanism. The literature review itself could be more evaluative by comparing objective and subjective sleep studies, asking whether cited deprivation findings generalise to natural sleep variation, and discussing measurement reliability of the dot-probe-like interference score. Bringing these issues into the rationale would make the criticality more evenly distributed across the proposal.

## Academic Integrity — 76%

The main claims are consistently cited and the reference list provides clear traceability. You distinguish direct measurement from inference and keep the observational design separate from causal language. Continue to check that each methodological claim—such as the recommendation for at least five actigraphy nights—is accurately represented by the source and that terminology for sex is consistent throughout. Because biological sex is treated as a moderator, the final participant materials should also explain how that variable is collected and why, while avoiding language that implies a simple binary mechanism where the underlying biology is more complex.
