## Research Methodology and Appropriate Implementation — 70%

The cross-sectional moderation design is well aligned with the question. These are not minor details: they affect measurement equivalence, recruitment duration and whether the final sample can meet the power target. Resolve them before the study is treated as fully feasible.

## Ethical Considerations — 76%

This is an excellent ethics section for a sensitive topic. You identify sexual functioning, intimate relationships and possible trauma as areas requiring careful consent and risk management, while explicitly avoiding the assumption that veteran status itself means vulnerability. One point to clarify is how anonymity interacts with withdrawal after survey submission, and whether any IP, platform metadata or contact information will be collected. State this explicitly so participants understand the practical limits of withdrawal in an anonymous online design.

## Criticality — 76%

Your criticality is consistently strong. You challenge penetration-centred definitions of successful sex, distinguish a self-reported EHS score from a direct physiological record, recognise reverse causation between satisfaction and communication, and acknowledge that communication is dyadic even though only one partner is measured. Veteran sexual functioning can be influenced by medication, injury, chronic disease and PTSD symptoms, yet you deliberately minimise collection of health information for sensitivity reasons. Explain how that ethical choice limits adjustment and therefore the interpretation of the moderation result.
