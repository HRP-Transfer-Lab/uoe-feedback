## Research Methodology and Appropriate Implementation — 63%

The randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled 2 × 2 mixed design is appropriate and well described. PHQ-9 and RMSSD are operationalised with validated measures, HRV standardisation is detailed, and group × time interactions directly test H1 and H2. The fallback to non-parametric tests also needs care because separate Mann–Whitney and Wilcoxon tests are not a direct replacement for a group × time interaction. A mixed-effects model could provide a more coherent framework for incomplete repeated data and intention-to-treat analysis.

## Ethical Considerations — 76%

Ethical reasoning is a major strength. You recognise that an intervention study creates risks beyond ordinary survey research, including digestive adverse effects and the PHQ-9 item on suicidal thoughts. The information sheet should also be fully consistent with the retention and withdrawal period. These additions would make an already thoughtful ethics section operationally complete.

## Criticality — 68%

You show good critical awareness by distinguishing human RCT evidence from animal vagotomy evidence, recognising smaller probiotic effects in non-clinical samples, and repeatedly limiting claims about vagal mechanism. The limitations acknowledge measurement imprecision, self-report, power, adherence and the practical difficulty of an eight-week blinded supplementation trial. The proposal could deepen its criticality by discussing strain-specificity and heterogeneity more explicitly: “probiotics” are not one intervention, so findings from other formulations may not generalise to the exact product you select. You should also consider expectancy, dietary change and other concurrent behaviours as alternative explanations for symptom change even under blinding.
