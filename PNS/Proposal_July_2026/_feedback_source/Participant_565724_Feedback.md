## Research Methodology and Appropriate Implementation — 37%

There are important design and analysis inconsistencies that must be resolved. The abstract states that the test group receives allotted meal times, while the Design section says the control group has standardised meal times and the test group does not. The sample of 256 is described as giving standard power for a medium effect, but the actual power calculation and assumptions are not provided. These issues substantially weaken methodological coherence.

Because eating schedules can interact with health conditions and medication, the risk assessment and exclusion criteria need a more explicit evidence-based rationale.

## Criticality — 37%

The proposal contains some reflective points, particularly around individual differences, self-report bias and life stressors that could affect wellbeing. The proposal also needs to distinguish sex-related hypotheses from gendered dietary behaviour more carefully. Stronger criticality would compare the quality and relevance of the cited studies, explain alternative mechanisms and show how the design or analysis can address the most important confounds.
