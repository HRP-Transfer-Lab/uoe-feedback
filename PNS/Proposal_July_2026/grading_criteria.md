# PNS Research Proposal — Subcriterion Scoring Rubric

Version 1.0 | 19 September 2026

## 1. Purpose and assessment structure

This rubric operationalises the University of Essex Online Psychobiology & Neuroscience Research Proposal Report criteria for Stage 1 cohort scoring.

| Criterion | Weight | Subcriteria |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| C1 Knowledge and Understanding of the Topic / Issues Under Consideration | 20% | 5 |
| C2 Knowledge and Critical Understanding of Research Methodology and Appropriate Implementation | 20% | 6 |
| C3 Ethical Considerations | 20% | 5 |
| C4 Criticality | 10% | 5 |
| C5 Academic Integrity | 10% | 4 |
| C6 Use of Relevant Sources | 10% | 5 |
| C7 Structure and Presentation | 10% | 5 |

There are 35 subcriteria in total.

The assignment is a 2,000-word quantitative research proposal applying psychobiology, cognitive neuroscience or neuropsychology to cognitive functioning or wellbeing. The proposal should include an Introduction, Research Design, Timeline, References and Appendices.

Do not introduce hidden requirements that are not in the assessment brief. In particular:
- an explicit null hypothesis is not compulsory;
- a separate Expected Results section is not compulsory;
- a separate Discussion section is not compulsory.

Relevant critical interpretation, implications and limitations can be demonstrated wherever they occur in the proposal.

## 2. Internal Stage 1 scoring

Score every subcriterion using only:

**1 = Fully demonstrated** — clearly present, substantively correct and appropriately applied to the student's own proposed study. Minor imperfections that do not materially weaken the competence should not prevent a score of 1.

**0.5 = Partially demonstrated** — recognisably present, but incomplete, insufficiently justified, inconsistent, vague in an important respect, or containing a meaningful conceptual or methodological weakness.

**0 = Not demonstrated** — absent, seriously inaccurate, inappropriate for the proposed study, contradicted elsewhere in the proposal, or too underdeveloped to establish the competence.

A recurring rule is: **naming something is not the same as demonstrating it.**

At Stage 1 do not assign percentage marks, grade bands, final grades or student-facing feedback. Score all papers independently first, then complete a cross-cohort calibration pass before conversion.

# 3. Criterion 1 — Knowledge and Understanding of the Topic / Issues — 20%

## C1A — Psychobiological/neuropsychological knowledge
**1:** Accurately explains both the relevant biological, neural, physiological or neuropsychological component and the cognitive, behavioural or wellbeing component at sufficient depth to establish the scientific basis of the proposal.
**0.5:** Both components are identifiable, but one is treated superficially, contains minor conceptual inaccuracies, or the relation between constructs is insufficiently developed.
**0:** The proposal is essentially psychological with no meaningful biological/neuropsychological dimension, or contains major misunderstandings of the central constructs.

## C1B — Mechanistic/theoretical rationale
**1:** Provides a plausible, literature-supported explanatory pathway connecting the biological factor with the psychological outcome and uses it to motivate the proposed relationship.
**0.5:** A plausible mechanism or theory is mentioned but the explanatory chain is incomplete, largely asserted, or only weakly connected to the proposed variables.
**0:** Gives no meaningful mechanism/theoretical explanation, or relies only on statements that previous studies found X associated with Y.

## C1C — Literature gap or justified extension
**1:** Clearly identifies a specific and bounded uncertainty, inconsistency, limitation or extension arising from previous research and shows how the proposed study addresses it.
**0.5:** Indicates that further research is needed or identifies a broad gap, but does not clearly establish precisely what remains unknown or why the proposed study fills it.
**0:** No identifiable literature-derived gap/extension, or novelty consists merely of claiming the topic has not been studied enough.

Suitable gaps can include population, context, boundary condition, mechanism, measurement, triangulation, replication or clarification of inconsistent evidence.

## C1D — Research question, aims and objectives
**1:** Provides a clearly answerable research question plus compatible aim/objectives, identifying the population/context where relevant, key variables and proposed relationship.
**0.5:** The broad question is understandable but important elements are ambiguous, over-broad, duplicated across multiple loosely related aims, or inconsistently expressed.
**0:** No clear research question or the stated question cannot realistically be answered by the proposed study.

## C1E — Hypothesis and conceptual alignment
**1:** Gives a clear, testable hypothesis or hypotheses derived from the rationale and appropriately specifies direction where theory justifies one. Hypothesis, question and variables are aligned.
**0.5:** A testable prediction is present but incompletely specified, weakly justified, unnecessarily non-directional despite clear prior evidence, or somewhat inconsistent with the stated question/design.
**0:** No testable hypothesis where one is appropriate, or the stated hypothesis does not correspond to the variables/design.

An explicit null hypothesis is not required.

# 4. Criterion 2 — Knowledge and Critical Understanding of Research Methodology and Appropriate Implementation — 20%

## C2A — Design–question alignment
**1:** Correctly identifies and justifies a design capable of answering the research question, with causal language appropriately matched to experimental versus observational evidence.
**0.5:** Broad design choice is workable, but its classification, justification or implications for inference are incompletely understood.
**0:** Design cannot answer the proposed question, is seriously misclassified, or supports substantially weaker inference than the student claims.

## C2B — Operationalisation and measurement
**1:** Clearly distinguishes constructs from their measures and specifies how the principal biological/neuropsychological and psychological variables will be quantified or classified.
**0.5:** Measures are named but operational definitions are incomplete, scores/outcomes are unclear, or one major construct is much better operationalised than the other.
**0:** Key constructs remain unmeasured or the proposed measure does not plausibly operationalise the stated construct.

## C2C — Quality and justification of measures
**1:** Selects appropriate measures and provides relevant methodological or validation evidence for important instruments/tasks, while recognising important measurement limitations where necessary.
**0.5:** Measures appear broadly suitable but validation, reliability, construct validity or methodological justification is limited or uneven.
**0:** A major measure is inappropriate, insufficiently described, or its validity for the intended construct is seriously doubtful.

## C2D — Participants and sampling
**1:** Defines the target population, sampling/recruitment strategy and relevant inclusion/exclusion criteria and provides a reasonable justification for sample size or power.
**0.5:** Population and recruitment are broadly clear but sample-size justification, eligibility, representativeness or recruitment feasibility is underdeveloped.
**0:** Participant population is inadequately defined, recruitment is unrealistic, or no defensible basis for the proposed sample is evident.

## C2E — Data-analysis plan
**1:** Correctly identifies predictors/independent variables, outcomes/dependent variables and relevant grouping/moderating variables and selects statistical analyses that directly test the hypotheses; important assumptions or planned comparisons are addressed where appropriate.
**0.5:** Main analysis is broadly appropriate but incompletely specified, poorly linked to hypotheses, or contains a remediable statistical misunderstanding.
**0:** Analysis is absent, fundamentally inappropriate, or cannot test the stated hypothesis.

Correct alignment matters more than unnecessary statistical complexity.

## C2F — Procedure, feasibility and implementation
**1:** Provides a sufficiently reproducible procedural outline and proposes a study realistic for the available population, equipment, access, expertise and timeframe.
**0.5:** Study is broadly feasible but important practical details or constraints remain unresolved.
**0:** Major elements are not implementable as proposed or the procedural description is too incomplete to judge implementation.

Feasibility should be judged against realistic online MSc constraints rather than an assumed fully resourced neuroscience laboratory.

# 5. Criterion 3 — Ethical Considerations — 20%

## C3A — Autonomy and valid consent
**1:** Clearly addresses voluntary informed consent, withdrawal and relevant autonomy issues, including capacity, vulnerability, incentives, unequal relationships or coercion where relevant.
**0.5:** Covers standard consent/withdrawal but gives limited consideration to study-specific autonomy issues.
**0:** Consent is absent, materially inadequate, or important foreseeable autonomy/coercion issues are ignored.

## C3B — Privacy, confidentiality and data governance
**1:** Explains what data are collected, whether they are anonymous, pseudonymous or identifiable, how confidentiality is maintained and how data will be stored, accessed, retained and ultimately destroyed or archived.
**0.5:** Mentions confidentiality and secure storage but leaves important elements unspecified or confuses anonymity with confidentiality.
**0:** Provides no credible data-management/privacy plan or proposes practices that materially compromise participant confidentiality.

## C3C — Risk, harm and participant welfare
**1:** Identifies plausible physical, psychological or social risks specific to the study, evaluates them proportionately and specifies sensible mitigation, stopping, debriefing or support procedures.
**0.5:** Recognises obvious risks but provides only generic safeguards or overlooks a significant but manageable issue.
**0:** Material risks are ignored or inadequately managed.

## C3D — Method-specific psychobiological/neuroethical issues
**1:** Correctly identifies and manages ethics issues specifically generated by the chosen method, measure, intervention or participant group, or appropriately explains why no substantial method-specific issue arises beyond standard procedures.
**0.5:** Shows awareness of a relevant issue but treatment is incomplete.
**0:** Ignores an important foreseeable issue, for example health-data interpretation, pharmacological risk, vulnerable neurological populations, incidental information, physiological burden or inappropriate diagnostic implications.

Do not reward students for inventing neuroethical problems in a simple minimal-risk study.

## C3E — Ethical reasoning and research value
**1:** Moves beyond procedural compliance by explicitly balancing scientific value, participant burden, risk/benefit and relevant ethical principles.
**0.5:** Shows some ethical reasoning but mainly lists safeguards rather than explaining why they are proportionate or justified.
**0:** Ethics consists almost entirely of boilerplate statements with no application to the proposed research.

# 6. Criterion 4 — Criticality — 10%

## C4A — Critical synthesis of existing research
**1:** Compares findings, theories or methodologies and evaluates differences in evidence rather than describing studies sequentially.
**0.5:** Contains some comparison/evaluation but remains substantially descriptive.
**0:** Literature review is essentially a succession of summaries or claims with little critical engagement.

## C4B — Evaluation of inference
**1:** Appropriately distinguishes what studies establish from stronger interpretations, including correlation/causation, direct versus indirect measures and limits of neural/physiological inference.
**0.5:** Shows some caution about inference but applies it inconsistently.
**0:** Repeatedly treats associations as causal, proxies as direct measures, or neural evidence as establishing more than the design supports.

## C4C — Alternative explanations and confounds
**1:** Identifies important confounds, alternative mechanisms or rival explanations and explains how the design or analysis will address them, or how they constrain interpretation.
**0.5:** Relevant alternatives/confounds are named but not adequately integrated into design or interpretation.
**0:** Major obvious alternative explanations are ignored.

## C4D — Critical evaluation of the proposed study
**1:** Identifies meaningful study-specific limitations and explains what each limitation affects, for example construct validity, internal validity, statistical inference, ecological validity or generalisability.
**0.5:** Relevant limitations are identified but mostly listed rather than evaluated.
**0:** Limitations are absent, generic or clearly inappropriate to the study.

Judge this at the level reasonably achievable in a 2,000-word MSc proposal, not against the detail expected in a journal protocol.

## C4E — Significance and justified implications
**1:** Explains precisely how possible findings could extend, clarify, constrain or challenge existing knowledge and keeps practical/theoretical claims within the inferential limits of the design.
**0.5:** Importance is plausible but broad, somewhat overstated or incompletely connected to the literature gap.
**0:** Significance is largely asserted or implications substantially exceed what the proposed design could establish.

# 7. Criterion 5 — Academic Integrity — 10%

Do not infer plagiarism, misconduct or unauthorised AI use from prose style. Formal academic-integrity investigation is separate from ordinary marking.

## C5A — Attribution of evidence and ideas
**1:** Empirical findings, theoretical claims, measures and other authors' ideas are consistently attributed to appropriate sources.
**0.5:** Attribution is generally sound but there are noticeable unsupported or ambiguously attributed claims.
**0:** Substantial reliance on unattributed material or repeated presentation of others' ideas as unsupported fact.

## C5B — Citation–reference correspondence
**1:** In-text citations and reference-list entries correspond consistently with no meaningful pattern of missing references.
**0.5:** Several correspondence errors or omissions occur but the scholarly trail remains broadly recoverable.
**0:** Extensive mismatch or incompleteness materially undermines source traceability.

## C5C — APA scholarly conventions
**1:** APA 7 citation and reference conventions are consistently and accurately applied with, at most, minor slips.
**0.5:** Recognisably follows APA but contains repeated inconsistencies or errors.
**0:** Referencing conventions are substantially absent or seriously inaccurate.

## C5D — Evidential transparency
**1:** Clearly distinguishes prior evidence, theoretical interpretation, methodological assumptions and the student's own predictions/proposals.
**0.5:** Generally distinguishable but some assertions blur evidence, interpretation and prediction.
**0:** These categories are repeatedly conflated.

# 8. Criterion 6 — Use of Relevant Sources — 10%

## C6A — Range and relevance of reading
**1:** Uses sufficient literature to establish the relevant biological/neuropsychological construct, psychological outcome, proposed relationship and study rationale.
**0.5:** Reading is broadly relevant but narrow, uneven or leaves an important component weakly supported.
**0:** Evidence base is markedly insufficient or substantially off-topic.

Do not impose a hidden minimum number of references.

## C6B — Academic quality of sources
**1:** Important claims predominantly rely on appropriate peer-reviewed research, scholarly reviews/theory or authoritative professional sources.
**0.5:** Mostly credible literature but with noticeable dependence on weaker secondary/non-academic material for substantive claims.
**0:** Important claims rely substantially on unsuitable or non-scholarly sources where appropriate academic literature is available.

## C6C — Current and foundational literature
**1:** Uses recent evidence where the field requires it while retaining relevant foundational theories/studies where necessary.
**0.5:** Literature is somewhat dated or overly recent without appropriate theoretical foundations, but remains usable.
**0:** Important developments or foundational work are substantially absent in a way that distorts the rationale.

## C6D — Methodological sources
**1:** Important measures, tasks, physiological indices and analysis/design choices are supported with relevant methodological or validation sources.
**0.5:** Some methodological choices are supported but major instruments/indices remain weakly justified.
**0:** Methodological selections are largely unsupported.

## C6E — Integration of sources
**1:** Sources are compared and synthesised to construct an argument leading towards the gap, mechanism and design.
**0.5:** Some synthesis is evident but much of the review remains source-by-source.
**0:** Sources are predominantly listed or summarised independently without building a coherent rationale.

# 9. Criterion 7 — Structure and Presentation — 10%

## C7A — Required proposal architecture
**1:** Includes the required Introduction, Research Design components, Timeline, References and relevant Appendices, with the major required elements readily identifiable.
**0.5:** Minor structural omissions or misplaced material, while the proposal remains readily interpretable.
**0:** Major required sections/elements are absent or organisation significantly obstructs assessment of the proposal.

## C7B — Logical organisation and coherence
**1:** Develops coherently from literature → rationale/gap → question/hypothesis → design → measurement/analysis → ethics/limitations.
**0.5:** Overall structure is understandable but transitions or ordering occasionally obscure the logic.
**0:** Structure is substantially fragmented or contradictory.

## C7C — Clarity and concision
**1:** Writing is clear, economical and appropriately detailed for a 2,000-word proposal, with little unnecessary repetition.
**0.5:** Generally understandable but contains noticeable verbosity, vagueness, repetition or under-explanation.
**0:** Writing substantially obstructs comprehension.

## C7D — Timeline, figures, tables and appendices
**1:** Supporting material is clear, relevant and professionally integrated, and the timeline is realistic for the proposed study.
**0.5:** Supporting material is present but somewhat incomplete, unclear, weakly integrated or the timeline contains minor feasibility problems.
**0:** Required timeline is absent/unworkable or supporting material substantially fails to serve its purpose.

## C7E — Professional academic presentation
**1:** Headings, layout, spelling, grammar and overall visual presentation are consistently suitable for postgraduate academic work.
**0.5:** Presentation is broadly professional but includes repeated minor weaknesses.
**0:** Frequent presentation or written-language problems materially reduce readability/professionalism.

Do not score APA referencing again here; it is already assessed under C5C.

# 10. Cross-cutting calibration rules

1. Score the competence wherever it appears; do not require it to occur beneath a particular heading.
2. Do not punish the same defect repeatedly unless it genuinely affects different competences.
3. Use 0.5 for genuine but incomplete competence; this is not a binary checklist.
4. Judge at Master's-proposal level, not published-paper level.
5. Do not reward complexity for its own sake.
6. Do not use cohort position when assigning Stage 1 codes; score evidence first, calibrate consistency afterwards.

# 11. Within-criterion weighting

Subcriteria are equally weighted within each parent criterion only.

- C1: each subcriterion = 1/5 of C1
- C2: each subcriterion = 1/6 of C2
- C3: each subcriterion = 1/5 of C3
- C4: each subcriterion = 1/5 of C4
- C5: each subcriterion = 1/4 of C5
- C6: each subcriterion = 1/5 of C6
- C7: each subcriterion = 1/5 of C7

Do not add all 35 raw subcriterion points together to determine the grade. The official criterion weights must be applied only after each criterion has been normalised to a common 0–1 scale.

# 12. Word-count penalty

The word-count penalty is external to this competence rubric and external to the cohort conversion.

The permitted maximum before penalty is 2,200 words. If the submission exceeds this limit, first calculate the academically derived grade in full. Then apply the prescribed 10 grade-point deduction as a separately recorded post-conversion penalty.

Do not lower C7, any other criterion, or the Stage 1 competence codes merely because the proposal exceeds the word-count threshold. This prevents double penalisation.
