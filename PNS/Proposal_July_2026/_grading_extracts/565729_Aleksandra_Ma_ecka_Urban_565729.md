[0029] [Normal] Cardiac Interoceptive Accuracy, ADHD Traits, and Flexible Regulation of Emotional Expression in Adults

[0030] [Normal] Background Literature Review

[0032] [Normal] Introception can be defined as the ability to sense, process, and represent physiological signals from within the body, including respiratory, cardiovascular, and gastrointestinal signals (Khalsa et al., 2018). Across different dimensions, the differences in introception may be conceptualised, which includes introceptive accuracy – reflecting objectively measured performance in detecting signals from the body, where introceptive sensibility refers more to self-reported perception and beliefs concerning internal states of a person (Garfinkel et al., 2015). Cardiac introceptive accuracy is usually assessed through performance-based heartbeat perception tasks. Next, neuroimaging evidence indicates that cardiac introceptive processing involves regions which are including the insular and anterior cingulate cortices, with right anterior insula activity associated with heartbeat detection accuracy (Critchley et al., 2004). Cardiac interoceptive accuracy may be relevant to emotion regulation, as interoceptive representations contribute to emotional experience and interpretation (Critchley & Garfinkel, 2017).

[0034] [Normal] Rising evidence suggesting that cardiac interoceptive accuracy can contribute to emotion regulation, even though the relationship is not well established. Greater heartbeat perception accuracy may be associated with more effective down-regulation of negative affect during cognitive reappraisal (Füstös et al., 2013). For example, Schuette et al. (2021) found that self-reported dimensions of introception predicted emotion identification and regulation, whereas objectively measured heartbeat perception accuracy did not. Pinna and Edwards (2020) presented a systematic review and identified only three studies examining introception in relation to emotion regulation, underscoring the limited and inconsistent current evidence base.

[0036] [Normal] Critchley & Garfinkel (2017) proposed one possible mechanism: accurate representation of physiological change may provide information about the emergence or alteration of emotional states, contributing to regulatory feedback about whether an emotional response is changing and whether regulatory responses should be adjusted. This does not imply that greater interoceptive accuracy is inherently adaptive; rather, cardiac information can constitute one source of feedback whose usefulness depends on its interpretation alongside contextual information. Significantly, existing research has largely examined particular regulatory strategies rather than whether interoceptive accuracy supports adaptation of emotional responses across changing situational demands.

[0038] [Normal] Prior Findings

[0039] [Normal] Traits of ADHD can provide a relevant individual-differences context in which to examine these processes, as ADHD and ADHD-related traits have been associated with both difficulties in emotion regulation and altered interoceptive processing. One of the most recent systematic reviews has found that poor interoceptive functioning was in general associated with more ADHD symptoms, including hyperactivity, inattention and impulsivity. Nevertheless, findings vary across measures and studies and were limited by methodological heterogeneity (Bruton et al., 2025). Evidence for emotion-regulation difficulties is more consistent: a meta-analysis of adults with ADHD reported greater overall emotion dysregulation than in non-ADHD controls, and a moderate-to-strong link between ADHD symptom severity and emotion dysregulation (Beheshti et al., 2020).

[0040] [Normal] Nevertheless, the literaturę has mainly conceptualised emotional functioning in terms of dysregulation and strategy use, as systematic-review evidence indicates methodological heterogeneity and inconsistent findings across specific regulatory strategies (Soler- Gutiérrez et al., 2023). Furthermore, relationships between emotion regulation, interoception, and ADHD have been investigated, but separately. Therefore, it remains unclear whether ADHD traits can contribute to expressive flexibility when objectively measured cardiac introceptive accuracy is considered simultaneously. A dimensional approach also allows ADHD-related traits to be examined in a non-clinical sample, where questionnaire scores are not treated as evidence of diagnosis.

[0041] [Normal] Theory

[0042] [Normal] Regulatory Flexibility Theory offers a broader perspective, proposing that adaptive regulation depends on adjusting regulatory responses to changing situational demands, rather than relying on strategies assumed to be inherently adaptive or maladaptive (Bonnano & Burton, 2013). The framework identifies three interacting components: access to a repertoire of regulatory strategies, sensitivity to contextual demands, and responsiveness to feedback about their effectiveness. Bonanno and Burton (2013) have noted that sensitivity may provide a theoretical link between interoception and regulatory flexibility.

[0043] [Normal] More accurate detection of changes in bodily state may be providing more accurate information about changes in internal state during an emotional response. It can also potentially support decisions about whether an expressive response should be modified. Expressive flexibility can be examined using the Flexible Regulation of Emotional Expression (FREE) Scale, which assesses the ability to enhance and suppress emotional expression across different situational contexts (Burton & Bonanno, 2016). Expressive flexibility refers to the capacity to modify emotional expression in accordance with contextual demands, rather than treating enhancement or suppression as uniformly adaptive.

[0045] [Normal] Therefore, cardiac interoceptive accuracy can represent one component of the regulatory system, but cannot represent a sufficient basis for flexibility itself. However, examination of cardiac interoceptive accuracy alongside ADHD traits allows physiological and psychological individual differences that can contribute to expressive abilities to be considered simultaneously.

[0047] [Normal] Rationale and Hypotheses

[0048] [Normal] ADHD traits may contribute independently to expressive flexibility, given their associations with introceptive processing and emotion dysregulation (Beheshti et al., 2020; Bruton et al., 2025). Nevertheless, it remains unclear whether objective measures of cardiac introceptive accuracy and dimensional ADHD traits can account for variation in flexible suppression and enhancement of emotional expression independently. The presented study aims to address this gap by examining both as simultaneous predictors of enhancement and suppression abilities, which will be analysed separately, in a non-clinical sample.

[0049] [Normal] The study aims to address the following research question:

[0051] [Normal] To what extent do cardiac introceptive accuracy and ADHD traits predict flexible regulation of emotional expression in adults?

[0053] [Normal] Taking into account evidence linking cardiac interoceptive accuracy with emotion regulation (Füstös et al., 2013) and the proposed role of bodily-state sensitivity in regulatory flexibility (Bonanno & Burton, 2013), the study hypothesised that:

[0055] [Normal] H1: Greater cardiac interoceptive accuracy will predict higher perceived enhancement and suppression ability.

[0057] [Normal] Given evidence linking ADHD symptoms with emotion-regulation difficulties (Beheshti et al., 2020), it is further hypothesised that:

[0059] [Normal] H2: Higher ADHD traits will predict lower perceived enhancement and suppression ability.

[0061] [Normal] The association between ADHD traits and cardiac interoceptive accuracy will be examined exploratorily because findings in the existing literature are inconsistent (Bruton et al., 2025).

[0063] [Normal] Aims and Objectives

[0064] [Normal] The primary aim of the proposed study is to investigate whether ADHD traits and cardiac introceptive accuracy will predict individual differences in the flexible regulation of emotional expression in an adult sample. The study will especially focus on: (1) examining whether cardiac introceptive accuracy predicts perceived enhancement and suppression ability; (2) examining whether ADHD traits on their own predict these outcomes when considered alongside cardiac interoceptive accuracy; and (3) studying the association between ADHD traits and cardiac introceptive accuracy.

[0066] [Normal] Research Design

[0067] [Normal] Design

[0068] [Normal] This study will use a quantitative, cross-sectional correlational design to examine whether individual differences in cardiac introceptive accuracy and ADHD traits predict flexible regulation of emotional expression in an adult sample. ADHD traits and cardiac introceptive accuracy will constitute the predictor variables, while enhancement and suppression abilities will be separate outcome variables.

[0069] [Normal] Materials

[0070] [Normal] The study will use the Staircase-Heartbeat Discrimination Task (S-HDT; Savage et al., 2026; see Appendix F) to assess cardiac introceptive accuracy. S-HDT is a recent development of performance-based measure design that addresses recognised limitations of traditional heartbeat discrimination paradigms. To record cardiac activity, the study will use electrocardiography (ECG), which will provide R-wave timing against which participants’ heartbeat judgements can be referenced. The S-HDT adaptively varies the temporal delay between cardiac events and external stimuli according to participants’ responses, allowing estimation of cardiac interoceptive accuracy alongside the point of subjective simultaneity (PSS).

[0071] [Normal] Secondly, the study will assess flexible regulation of emotional expression with use of the Flexible Regulation of Emotional Expression (FREE) Scale (Burton & Bonanno, 2016; see Appendix E), with perceived enhancement and suppression ability scored separately. Continuing, the ADHD traits will be measured continuously with use of the 18-item Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale v1.1 (ASRS-v1.1; Kessler et al., 2005; see Appendix D). Scores range from 0 (Never) to 4 (Very Often) and will be summed to produce a continuous total score from 0 to 72, where higher scores indicate greater ADHD-related symptom traits (Brevik et al., 2020).

[0075] [Normal] Participants

[0076] [Normal] Participants will be 140 adults aged 18 years or over recruited from a non-clinical population. An a priori power analysis for multiple linear regression with two predictors, assuming a medium effect size (f² = .15), α = .05, and 80% power, indicated approximately 68 analysable participants. Because Savage et al. (2026) retained 22 of 42 participants (52.38%) after S-HDT exclusions, recruiting 140 participants should provide approximately 73 usable datasets while allowing for questionnaire or ECG-related data loss.

[0077] [Normal] Participants must have sufficient English proficiency to understand the task instructions and questionnaire items. Individuals reporting cardiovascular or neurological conditions that could substantially affect cardiac signalling or task performance will be excluded. ADHD diagnosis will not be required, as ADHD traits will be measured continuously using the ASRS-v1.1. Relevant medication use, particularly medication that may influence cardiovascular function, will be recorded and considered when interpreting cardiac measurements.

[0078] [Normal] Data Analysis Plan

[0079] [Normal] Before analysis, study will screen the data for missing values, outliers, incomplete questionnaires, and unusable S-HDT or ECG recordings. The S-HDT exclusion criteria will be applied before statistical analysis. Next, descriptive statistics will be calculated for all study variables.

[0080] [Normal] To evaluate the research hypotheses, two multiple linear regression analyses will be conducted. The first will test H1 and H2 using cardiac interoceptive accuracy and ADHD traits as simultaneous predictors of enhancement ability. The second will use the same predictors to examine suppression ability. This approach is appropriate because the study examines whether two continuous predictor variables independently predict continuous outcome variables.

[0081] [Normal] Prior to conducting the regressions, assumptions of linearity, homoscedasticity, normally distributed residuals, multicollinearity, and influential observations will be assessed. Statistical significance will be set at p < .05, and model fit, regression coefficients, and 95% confidence intervals will be reported.

[0082] [Normal] Finally, the association between ADHD traits and cardiac interoceptive accuracy will be examined exploratorily using Pearson’s correlation coefficient. If its assumptions are substantially violated, Spearman’s rank correlation will be used instead.

[0083] [Normal] Procedure

[0084] [Normal] After gathering consent from the participants, they will next complete demographic information and the questionnaires (ASRS-v1.1 and FREE). Next, the ECG electrodes will be attached, and participants will complete the S-HDT in a calm, quiet laboratory environment. While participants are completing the task, the cardiac activity will be continuously recorded, with ECG R-waves providing the temporal reference for heartbeat stimuli. The delay between cardiac events and external stimuli will be adaptively adjusted according to participants’ responses, following the S-HDT procedure described by Savage et al. (2026). The task will take approximately 30 minutes. After completing the task, ECG equipment will be removed, and participants will receive a written debrief explaining the purpose of the study and reminding them that ASRS-v1.1 scores are not equivalent to a clinical diagnosis.

[0092] [Normal] Limitations

[0093] [Normal] There are many limitations that should be considered in this study. First, the cross-sectional design precludes causal conclusions regarding the relationship between cardiac inroceptive accuracy and expressive flexibility. Second, the FREE scale relies on self-reported responses to hypothetical scenarios and cannot fully reflect regulatory behaviour in everyday situations, even though it provides a validated measure of perceived enhancement and suppression ability (Burton & Bonanno, 2016). Third, S-HTD is a relatively new measure with limited independent validation; it still needs further investigation,, especially regarding its psychometric properties (Savage et al.). The S-HDT analysis pipeline also excludes participants whose response patterns are classified as random or consistent with a non-interoceptive cognitive strategy, which can potentially result in data loss. If the likelihood of exclusion is systematically associated with ADHD traits, this could additionally bias estimates of the relationships examined. Moving forward, performance on a cardiac introception task represents one specific dimension and modality of introceptive functioning and should not be considered as a comprehensive measure of participants' broader introceptive abilities. Overall, the study can address issues with increased sensitivity but has limitations in generalisation to individual differences in a non-clinical sample when assessing ADHD dimensionally.

[0215] [Normal] Appendix F

[0217] [Normal] Staircase-Heartbeat Discrimination Task (S-HDT) Protocol and Materials

[0219] [Normal] Cardiac interoceptive accuracy will be assessed using the Staircase-Heartbeat Discrimination Task (S-HDT) developed by Savage et al. (2026).

[0220] [Normal] Cardiac activity will be continuously recorded using electrocardiography (ECG). ECG R-waves will provide the temporal reference for presentation of heartbeat-related auditory stimuli.

[0222] [Normal] The S-HDT uses an adaptive procedure in which participants judge the temporal relationship between their heartbeat and externally presented tones. During training, participants complete between 2 and 20 training trials, with each trial consisting of 10 tones presented in relation to every second heartbeat. Stimulus delays range from 0 to 600 ms in 50-ms increments.

[0223] [Normal] The adaptive staircase procedure consists of four blocks of 15 trials, initiated at different delays:

[0224] [Normal] 400A → 100A → 400B → 100B

[0225] [Normal] The temporal delay is adjusted according to participants' responses concerning whether the tones occurred before, after, or at approximately the same time as their perceived heartbeat. Confidence judgements are also collected as part of the task but will not constitute a primary variable in the proposed analyses.

[0226] [Normal] The task permits estimation of cardiac interoceptive performance and the point of subjective simultaneity (PSS). Cardiac interoceptive accuracy derived from the S-HDT will constitute the primary physiological predictor in the proposed regression analyses.

[0227] [Normal] Predefined S-HDT data-quality criteria will be applied before statistical analysis, following the published procedure described by Savage et al. (2026). Response patterns classified as consistent with random responding or a non-interoceptive cognitive strategy will be excluded from the primary analyses. The published simulated reference trajectories will be used as specified in the S-HDT analysis pipeline.
