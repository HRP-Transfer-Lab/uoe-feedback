[0035] [Normal] Prolonged Cortisol Exposure Effect on Memory Performance

[0036] [Normal] Background Literature Review

[0037] [Normal] Cortisol is the principal glucocorticoid produced by the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and plays an important role in the regulation of physiological processes and the body’s response to stress. This area of the brain is crucial for learning and memory; in particular, it supports memory formation, recall, and relational binding of information (Keresztes et al., 2020, p.1). Acute cortisol responses are adaptive and help the organism respond to challenging situations; however, prolonged HPA axis activation and sustained alterations in cortisol secretion have been associated with adverse effects on physical and psychological functioning (Stalder & Kirschbaum, 2012, p.).

[0038] [Normal] Prior Findings

[0039] [Normal] Prior studies investigating the impact of stress on memory retrieval suggest that stress impairs the retrieval of hippocampus-dependent memory (Zerbes et al., 2020, p.2710). However, studies on chronic cortisol exposure are scarce, highlighting an area for future research (Huthsteiner et al., 2026, p.2). Cross-sectional and longitudinal studies have shown an association between higher cortisol levels and hippocampal atrophy. Suggesting that chronic cortisol exposure can damage the hippocampus, which can impair cortisol downregulation and lead to chronic elevation (Lebedeva et al., 2018, p.492). Chronic cortisol exposure not only affects memory processes but also increases the risk of cardiovascular and psychological diseases, demonstrating the importance of studying the relationship between variables (Glienke & Piefke, 2017, p.2).

[0040] [Normal] Rationale and Hypotheses

[0041] [Normal] Accurate assessment of chronic cortisol exposure is important when examining its association with memory performance. Conventional measures obtained from blood, saliva or urine capture relatively short-term cortisol concentrations and are influenced by substantial diurnal variation (Wester & Rossum, 2015, p.1). In contrast, hair cortisol concentration (HCC) provides a retrospective measure of integrated cortisol secretion over months and therefore more closely aligns with chronic cortisol exposure. However, methodological variation in hair collection, processing and analysis means that HCC should be interpreted as an indirect measure of chronic cortisol exposure rather than chronic psychological stress (Martins-Silva et al., 2025).

[0042] [Normal] The present study therefore investigates the following research question: Is long-term cortisol exposure associated with verbal learning and memory performance in healthy adults?

[0043] [Normal] We hypothesize that higher HCC, reflecting greater long-term cortisol exposure, will be associated with poorer performance on the Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test (RAVLT). Specifically, the following hypotheses are proposed:

[0044] [Normal] H1: HCC will be significantly associated with poorer delayed-recall performance on the RAVLT.

[0045] [Normal] H2: HCC will be significantly associated with poorer overall verbal learning performance on the RAVLT, as indicated by a lower total learning score across the acquisition trials.

[0046] [Normal] The null hypothesis is that there will be no statistically significant association between HCC and either delayed-recall performance or overall verbal learning performance on the RAVLT.

[0047] [Normal] Aims and Objectives

[0048] [Normal] This study will therefore investigate the association between chronic cortisol exposure, indexed by HCC, and memory performance assessed using the RAVLT in healthy adults.

[0049] [Normal] Research Design

[0050] [Normal] Design

[0051] [Normal] We will use a cross-sectional correlational design to examine the association between individual differences in chronic cortisol exposure and memory performance. We will operationalize chronic cortisol exposure as hair cortisol concentration, and assess memory performance using the Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test. A correlational design is appropriate, as cortisol exposure cannot be ethically or practically manipulated; therefore, the study examines associations rather than causal effects.

[0052] [Normal] Materials

[0053] [Normal] Hair Cortisol Concentration (HCC)

[0054] [Normal] We will assess chronic cortisol exposure using HCC. Hair sampling is a relatively straightforward and minimally invasive procedure that can be conducted in an outpatient setting (Wester & Rossum, 2015, p.2). Although HCC does not provide a direct measure of psychological stress and may be influenced by methodological and hair-related factors, it offers a more appropriate measure of chronic cortisol exposure than a single blood or saliva sample, as these measures can be substantially affected by short-term physiological factors like acute stress exposure or timing of sample collection (Wester & Rossum, 2015, p.3). Therefore, HCC provides a comparatively stable measure of cumulative cortisol that is aligned with the study’s focus on chronic cortisol exposure and memory performance.

[0055] [Normal] Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test (RAVLT)

[0056] [Normal] Verbal learning and memory will be assessed using the Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test (1941), a widely used neuropsychological measure of episodic memory. The RAVLT is a quick, easy-to-administer, and efficient test for measuring cognitive abilities and detecting memory loss (Dias et al., 2021, p.389). The RAVLT involves repeated presentation and recall of a word list and provides measures of verbal learning, immediate recall, delayed recall, and recognition. The primary outcome measure for the present study will be the delayed recall score, as it indicates retention and retrieval of previously learned verbal information. The RAVLT will be administered and scored according to standardized procedures as outlined by Schmidt (1996). The RAVLT is a sensitive memory test; however, lifestyle factors can affect performance (Schoenberg et al., 2006, p. 694). Therefore, we will administer the following additional questionnaires to enhance validity.

[0057] [Normal] Trier Inventory for Chronic Stress (TICS-9-EN)

[0058] [Normal] We will assess chronic stress using the Trier Inventory for Chronic Stress-9-EN. The original Trier Inventory for Chronic Stress consists of 57 items; however, a new 9-item short version covers all nine subdomains and has been shown to accurately measure chronic stress, as it is theoretically based and shows strong reliability (Petrowski et al., 2020, p.6).

[0059] [Normal] Demographic and Health Questionnaire

[0060] [Normal] Participants will complete a researcher-developed demographic and health questionnaire, as shown in Appendix C. Information collected will include age, sex, educational attainment, relevant medical conditions, medication use, alcohol and nicotine use, and hair-treatment history. These variables have been shown to confound HCC and are pertinent to note (Wells et al., 2014, p.340). We will use health and medication questions primarily to assess eligibility and identify potential factors that may influence cortisol concentrations or cognitive performance.

[0061] [Normal] Participants

[0062] [Normal] The study will recruit healthy adults aged 18-65 years using convenience sampling. We will recruit participants through university research participation systems such as mailing lists and social media platforms, with the advertisement reflected in Appendix A. We will conduct a priori power analysis using G*Power to determine the required sample size for the primary multiple linear regression analysis. The analysis will examine whether HCC significantly predicts RAVLT delayed-recall performance while controlling for relevant covariates. Assuming a medium effect size (f2 = .15), an alpha level of .05, and statistical power of .80, the required sample size is approximately 98 participants. To allow for potential exclusions, incomplete data and unusable hair samples, the study will aim to recruit 110-120 participants.

[0063] [Normal] Participants will be eligible if they are aged 18-65 years, can provide informed consent, have sufficient scalp hair for cortisol analysis, and can understand and complete the questionnaires and RAVLT. Given the multifactorial determinants of cortisol regulation and cognitive performance, an exhaustive list of exclusion criteria is not feasible. Accordingly, the study will prioritize exclusion criteria considered most relevant to the interpretability of the proposed analysis.

[0064] [Normal] Data Analysis Plan

[0065] [Normal] We will analyze the data using IBM SPSS Statistics (2025). Before analysis, we will screen the dataset for missing data, data-entry errors, outliers, and violations of statistical assumptions. We will calculate descriptive statistics for demographic, cortisol, and cognitive variables. We will summarize continuous variables using means and standard deviations. Before conducting multiple linear regression, we will examine the distribution of HCC using histograms and Q-Q plots. If we observe substantial positive skewness, we will natural log-transform HCC.

[0066] [Normal] We will first conduct a Pearson correlation to examine the bivariate association between HCC and RAVLT delayed recall.

[0067] [Normal] The primary analysis will be a multiple linear regression examining whether HCC predicts RAVLT delayed-recall performance. We will enter RAVLT delayed recall as the dependent variable, with HCC as the primary predictor. Age, sex, substance use, medication, educational attainment and chronic stress will be included as covariates due to their potential associations with cortisol concentrations and cognitive performance. We will examine the HCC regression coefficient, 95% confidence interval, and associated p-value to determine whether cortisol exposure is significantly associated with delayed recall after adjustment for covariates. We will assess statistical significance using a two-tailed alpha level of 0.05, and report regression coefficients and 95% confidence intervals to indicate the magnitude and precision of any association.

[0068] [Normal] We will reject the null hypothesis if higher HCC is significantly associated with lower RAVLT delayed-recall performance. Given the cross-sectional design, a statistically significant association would not imply a causal relationship between cortisol exposure and memory performance, as the design does not permit causal inferences.

[0069] [Normal] Procedure

[0070] [Normal] Participants will first complete a brief demographic and health/lifestyle questionnaire, as reflected in Appendix C. If they meet the inclusion criteria, participants will attend a scheduled research session after providing informed consent. Hair samples will be collected with scissors, and approximately 3cm of the proximal hair segment will be analyzed to provide an estimate of cortisol exposure over the preceding several months (Wester & Rossum, 2015, p.2).  Participants will subsequently complete the RAVLT, administered according to standardized instructions. The session will take approximately 30-45 minutes. After completion, participants will receive debrief information, including their right to withdraw, study details, available support, and researcher contact details.

[0074] [Normal] Limitations

[0075] [Normal] The cross-sectional design is a significant limitation for causal inference, as cortisol and memory performance are assessed at approximately the same time. Although this design may identify an association between cortisol concentrations and memory performance, it cannot establish whether chronically elevated cortisol contributes to poorer memory performance. A longitudinal design incorporating repeated assessments of cortisol and memory across multiple time points would provide stronger evidence regarding the relationship.  Future research could incorporate sequential hair cortisol concentration samples to provide repeated measures of cortisol exposure over extended periods, offering a more comprehensive assessment.

[0076] [Normal] Hair cortisol is an imperfect measure of long-term cortisol exposure. Although hair cortisol is useful for estimating longer-term cortisol exposure, it isn’t a perfect representation of an individual’s cortisol activity. Hair characteristics and external factors can influence cortisol measurements, such as hair treatments, chemical processing, hair colour, washing practices, cosmetic products, and individual differences in hair growth (Wester & Rossum, 2015, p.6). Future research should strengthen cortisol measurement by using other biomarkers, such as blood, saliva, or urine, to provide a more comprehensive measure of HPA activity.

[0077] [Normal] The RAVLT assesses specific components of memory, primarily verbal learning and episodic memory and therefore does not provide a comprehensive measure of memory functioning. Consequently, findings should be interpreted in terms of the relationship between cortisol and verbal learning and memory performance, rather than generalized to overall memory functioning. Furthermore, using multifactorial measures that assess multiple dimensions of cognitive functioning and generate a range of scores, rather than relying solely on a single global score, may enhance the validity and specificity of research findings (Vakil & Blachstein, 1993, p.889).

[0078] [Normal] Convenience sampling may limit the generalizability of the findings to the wider population. Participants recruited through convenience sampling may differ from the broader population in characteristics that are relevant to both cortisol levels and cognitive functioning. For example, recruiting predominantly university students may yield a sample with a narrow age range, similar backgrounds, and similar socioeconomic status. Future research can address these sampling limitations by expanding the sample to increase diversity and strengthen external validity.
