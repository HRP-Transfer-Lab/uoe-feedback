[0087] [Normal] Do males and females differ in autonomic responses during mental-state recognition during the Reading the Mind in the Eye Test, and is the magnitude of this physiological response associated with recognition accuracy?

[0090] [Normal] Background Literature Review

[0092] [Normal] Social cognition is the psychological theory of how people assess and interpret the social world around them. Two components of social cognition are cognitive empathy and the Theory of Mind (ToM).

[0093] [Normal] For this study, we will primarily use the term social cognition. References to cognitive empathy will also be made, which is the ability to infer and understand what someone else might be thinking or feeling from their perspective.

[0094] [Normal] The Theory of Mind principle was invented by David Premack and Guy Woodruff in 1978 and is a collective term for a person’s ability to comprehend and attribute another’s mental states, including their emotions, beliefs, knowledge or intentions. For this study, the term mentalizing will be referenced where needed.

[0096] [Normal] Reading the Mind in the Eye Test (RMET) is a psychological assessment which aims to assess an individual’s social cognition skills, including their cognitive empathy and Theory of Mind capabilities. It was created by Simon Baron-Cohen in 1997 at the University of Cambridge and is available online for people to test themselves.

[0098] [Normal] The RMET consists of 36 black-and-white photographs of male and female eyes with 4 multiple-choice answers consisting of one-word emotional/mental state descriptors. The participant attributes a mental state to the person in the photograph and is given an overall score out of 36.

[0102] [Normal] Prior Findings

[0104] [Normal] On average, females score slightly higher than males on the RMET, which has been further

[0105] [Normal] expounded in a 2022 study by the University of Cambridge, which recruited over 300,000 participants from 57 countries. (Greenberg et al., 2022)

[0107] [Normal] Extensive research (Christov-Moore et al., 2015; Proverbio et al., 2023; Nugent et al., 2011) has shown biological sex divergence in cognitive processes, accuracy and empathy during social cognition tasks.

[0109] [Normal] Males display higher activation and recruitment of prefrontal regions than females. Males are also more likely to exhibit co-activation of the parasympathetic and sympathetic during these tasks. Females demonstrate increased cardiac deceleration, indicative of parasympathetic activation and focused attention, during social cognition tasks in comparison to males.

[0111] [Normal] Research (Koenig and Thayer 2016) also shows that females have a higher resting parasympathetic activity than males, which results in a larger parasympathetic withdrawal in comparison to males before a sympathetic spike happens.

[0113] [Normal] There is conflicting research between studies that show biological sex differences in autonomic responses to emotional stimuli (Wilhelm et al., 2017) and studies (Šolcová and Lačev, 2017) that show no significant differences in autonomic responses between males and females.

[0115] [Normal] Research (Quintana et al., 2012) shows that participants who had a higher resting HRV, indicative of autonomic regulation, had higher RMET scores.

[0119] [Normal] Theory

[0121] [Normal] When the prefrontal cortex (PFC) is decoding complex mental states, acting as a vagal brake applied to the sympathetic nervous system, activating the parasympathetic nervous system, which results in a stable heart rate (HR) and increased heart rate variability (HRV).

[0123] [Normal] If the participant is distressed while decoding negative emotions, the PFC lifts the vagal brake and causes sympathetic activation, causing an increase in heart rate.

[0125] [Normal] A baseline of activation is necessary to recruit relevant cognitive functions for the task, as

[0126] [Normal] executive control and effortful attention are required for mentalizing.

[0128] [Normal] During social cognition tasks, significant sympathetic activation has a detrimental effect on accuracy, as personal distress and emotional dysregulation cause a reduction in the ability to successfully recruit and maintain sufficient cognitive resources, thus decreasing accuracy.

[0131] [Normal] Rationale and Hypothesis

[0133] [Normal] This study addresses the research question: Do males and females differ in autonomic responses, measured by HR and HRV, during mental state recognition during the Reading the Mind in the Eye Test correlate and is the magnitude of this physiological response associated with recognition accuracy?

[0135] [Normal] The inverted-U theory would hypothesise that males and females who display some autonomic arousal would result in improved scores due to optimal cognitive focus.

[0137] [Normal] However, an extreme increase, no increase or a decrease could predict lower scores, the former of which is suggestive of psychological distress, the latter suggestive of not recruiting sufficient cognitive resources for the task.

[0139] [Normal] As studies show (Bangasser et al., 2018), females are more vulnerable to autonomic arousal due to increased psychological stress, which could result in lower accuracy.

[0141] [Normal] Those with higher vagal tone at rest tend to score higher on social cognition tasks than those with lower vagal tone. Females could be at a slight advantage to the effects, as they typically have a higher vagal tone and so may not be as negatively impacted by increased arousal in comparison to males with lower vagal tone, who may have their cognitive processing capabilities more detrimentally impacted by a sympathetic spike.

[0143] [Normal] The correlative relationship between autonomic arousal and social cognition is Hypothesis A. To evaluate if there is a variation in this based on biological sex is Hypothesis B.

[0145] [Normal] The H0A is that there is no significant relationship between elevation of HR or increased HRV and accuracy of scores during the RMET.

[0146] [Normal] The H1A is that there is a significant relationship between elevation of HR or decreased HRV and accuracy of scores during the RMET.

[0148] [Normal] The H0B is that there is no significant difference in RMET score accuracy between males and females related to their HR/HRV changes during the test.

[0150] [Normal] The H1B is that there is a significant difference in RMET score accuracy between males and females related to HR/HRV changes during the test.

[0153] [Normal] Aims and Objectives

[0155] [Normal] Studies have compared RMET scores by biological sex, and other studies have looked at autonomic arousal when completing the RMET and other emotional facial recognition tasks. However, there is a gap in research that bridges these previous studies, where autonomic arousal is assessed when partaking in the RMET, and whether this differs by biological sex.

[0156] [Normal] This study will a) compare female score results to male test score results, b) compare females' heart rate and HRV changes compared to males', and c) assess if there is a correlation between autonomic arousal and score accuracy.

[0160] [Normal] Research Design

[0163] [Normal] Design

[0165] [Normal] This study is a mixed correlational study with a quantitative design to investigate the relationship between mentalizing/cognitive empathy and autonomic arousal and whether this differs by biological sex. Autonomic activity will be measured by HR and HRV changes during participation in the RMET compared to a resting baseline before the test commences.

[0167] [Normal] HR is a reliable marker of overall autonomic arousal, where a significantly raised HR could be indicative of arousal/distress resulting in lower score accuracy, or a moderate increase in HR could improve scores.

[0169] [Normal] HRV is a useful marker to evaluate cognitive processing recruitment. If there is a small drop in HRV during the task from baseline, it is indicative of the PFC releasing the vagal brake due to increased cognitive focus in the brain, which could lead to higher scores.

[0171] [Normal] If the drop is large, it is indicative of sympathetic activation, where distress and emotional dysregulation negatively impact cognitive recruitment.

[0174] [Normal] Materials

[0176] [Normal] The study will utilise the 2001 version of the RMET available online on the Embrace Autism website.

[0178] [Normal] Popular mainstream wearables do not provide unaggregated, raw data, only overnight averages, which is not suitable for this study. Due to these limitations, this study will use the Polar H10 Heart Rate Sensor, which is a chest strap that has built-in electrodes that record HR and HRV data by the millisecond at beat-to-beat intervals. As it is a chest strap, it minimises movement and muscle disturbance that can occur more often with wrist wearables.

[0180] [Normal] This will be paired with the Polar H10 ECG Heart Check App, which streams and saves data via Bluetooth.

[0182] [Normal] A preliminary questionnaire will be given to prospective participants to see if they are suitable for the study.

[0184] [Normal] Participants

[0186] [Normal] 25 males and 25 females (50 total participants) aged 20 to 40 will be recruited. A specific age group has been selected to increase uniformity of HR/HRV data typical of that group.

[0187] [Normal] Subject to completing the questionnaire, participants will be recruited on the basis that

[0188] [Normal] they do not have a formal diagnosis of an anxiety disorder or heart condition, are not taking certain prescription medications (e.g. tricyclic antidepressants or beta-blockers), and are not current smokers or taking any form of nicotine, as these conditions and medications can affect HR/HRV.

[0190] [Normal] Those who do not meet the criteria for this study will be excluded.

[0192] [Normal] Participants will be asked not to smoke, vape, drink coffee or alcohol on the morning of the study to ensure that stimulants do not artificially accelerate HR.

[0195] [Normal] Data Analysis Plan

[0197] [Normal] Prior to analysis, data preparation will be conducted to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the HR/HRV data collected. Any identifiable interruptions or anomalies that could skew the cardiac measurements, such as chest muscle interference (overcontraction or expansion) or particularly abnormal breathing patterns, will be addressed using standardised preprocessing techniques.

[0199] [Normal] The primary outcome/dependent variable in this study is the test accuracy score; the independent predictor variables are HR and HRV, and the moderator of this hypothesis is biological sex.

[0201] [Normal] The between-group factor is biological sex (males vs females), which will be processed as nominal data. To correlate autonomic arousal with overall accuracy of test scores, this will be processed using ratio data to process HR (BPM), HRV (time-domain, millisecond inter-beat intervals) and test accuracy (this will be out of 36, where 36 is 100% correct answers)

[0203] [Normal] Autonomic arousal will be calculated by subtracting the mean baseline from the mean task for both HR and HRV metrics. A positive HR would indicate an increase in autonomic arousal, and a positive HRV would indicate increased parasympathetic activity indicative of increased cognitive focus and regulatory capacity. A negative HR would indicate decreased autonomic arousal, and a negative HRV would indicate vagal withdrawal/ decrease in parasympathetic activity indicative of increased cognitive load, an increased stress response, moving into sympathetic activation and decreased regulatory capacity.

[0205] [Normal] The Shapiro-Wilk test will be used to check for normality.

[0207] [Normal] This study will rely on multiple linear regression and moderation analysis to assess if autonomic arousal relates to accuracy.

[0211] [Normal] Procedure

[0213] [Normal] Participants' baseline HR and HRV will be measured using the Polar H10 Heart Rate Sensor for 5 minutes sitting and staring at a fixed object before starting the RMET.

[0214] [Normal] They will complete the test, selecting one mental state/emotion out of four for the 36 images. Cardiac activity will continue to be recorded using the Polar H10 Heart Rate Sensor, connected via Bluetooth to the Polar H10 ECG Heart Check App software.

[0215] [Normal] Once completed, scores will be noted, corresponding to the participants' biological sex and HR/HRV data for processing.

[0228] [Normal] Limitations

[0230] [Normal] Some critics dispute that the RMET is useful for inferring complex mental states, saying it is instead an indicator of basic facial emotional recognition. The premise of this study hinges on the predominant view that the RMET is suitable for examining social cognition abilities.

[0232] [Normal] Another limitation is that varying English vocabulary knowledge could be an additional factor which could impact score accuracy.
